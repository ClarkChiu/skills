# Prioritization frameworks — one-page cheat-sheet

The five public methods the daily-brief engine composes. Consult this when a
classification or day-shaping call isn't obvious. Do NOT paste this into the brief —
the brief carries decisions, not theory.

## 1. Eisenhower matrix (urgent × important)
Classify every task on two axes — *urgent* (time-pressured) and *important* (moves a real
goal). The trap is treating urgent as important; most "urgent, not important" work is
someone else's priority leaking onto your list.

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **DO today** — the real load | **SCHEDULE** — name a day/slot, or it never happens |
| **Not important** | **DELEGATE / batch** — hand off or do in one cheap pass | **DROP** — say it out loud; don't pad the list |

Key move: the "important, not urgent" box is where the high-leverage work lives and where
it quietly dies. Give it a concrete slot, not "later."

## 2. Eat the Frog (Brian Tracy / Mark Twain)
From the DO box, pick the **single hardest or most-avoided important task** and do it
first, before the day's energy and attention erode. One frog, not three. If you'd
procrastinate on it, that's the signal it's the frog. Doing it first removes the dread tax
that otherwise drags on everything else.

## 3. The 1-3-5 rule (realistic daily shape)
A day holds about **1 big · 3 medium · 5 small** tasks — no more. It's a forcing function
against the fantasy to-do list. Everything beyond 1-3-5 is **explicitly deferred** (named
and pushed to a day), never silently dropped. The frog is usually the "1 big."

## 4. Subtraction pass (the part most lists skip)
Actively name **1–2 things to cut or say no to** today. A good brief subtracts, it doesn't
only add. Candidates: low-value recurring meetings, "nice to have" polish, work that's
really someone else's, anything you're doing out of habit rather than need. Saying no is a
deliverable.

## 5. Load check (time vs calendar)
Put a **rough time estimate** on each DO item, sum it, and compare against the day's fixed
`CALENDAR` commitments. If estimates + meetings exceed the working hours available, the day
is **over-committed** — say so plainly and name what to move, shrink, or drop. A plan that
doesn't fit the clock isn't a plan. (This is the number-heavy section — apply 盤古之白 in
Chinese: 「預估 3 小時」.)

## How they compose (the engine's order)
Review yesterday → triage email into the task pool → **Eisenhower** classify →
**Frog** pick from DO → **1-3-5** shape the day → **subtraction** name cuts →
**load check** against the calendar. Each step narrows; the output is one brief, not five
framework dumps.
